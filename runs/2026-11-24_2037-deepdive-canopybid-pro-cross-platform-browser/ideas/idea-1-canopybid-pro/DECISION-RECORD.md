## Root-Cause Persistence
- The proximate symptom—designers duct-taping Morpholio Trace (~$25/yr) + PlantMaster ($300/yr) + Excel or tolerating clunky $900-$1,629/yr desktop CAD—persists for two distinct root causes, only one of which is architectural debt (15-30 year old Windows/desktop C++ CAD codebases like DynaScape, PRO Landscape, and Vectorworks moving slowly on native iPad Apple Pencil UX). The deeper, NON-indie-favorable root causes (DP1, DP4) explain why a $828/yr cross-platform 2D-only unified studio has not won despite ten active vertical competitors: (1) Severe Buyer Bifurcation ("The 2D-Only Sandwich"): 1-5 person residential design-build contractors who actually pay $900-$1,600/yr increasingly require 3D/photo visuals to close $25k-$75k homeowner bids (iscapeit.com; structurestudios.com) and already have PRO Landscape+ at $900/yr (2D CAD + 3D + photo imaging + 19k plants + free iPad Companion; prolandscape.com) or Realtime Landscaping Architect at $599 one-time (ideaspectrum.com), whereas 2D-loyal Mac/iPad solo garden designers anchor at $100-$350/yr (Sow Pro+ $99.99/yr, PlantMaster $300/yr, Shoot Gardening ~£108-£240/yr, gCADPlus $95 one-time). (2) Copyrighted Multi-Region Horticultural Data Moat & Dual-Product Scope: Scaling to Plausible = 100,000 requires legally cleared regional cultivar + photo databases across US USDA, Canada, UK RHS, Australia, and Western Europe; plant photography is heavily litigated (netpsplantfinder.com), NetPS Plus ($1,895+/yr + setup) licenses single-retailer e-commerce feeds rather than multi-tenant SaaS PDF redistribution and revokes image rights on cancellation, RHS restricts its 35k-plant database and Lindley imagery to selective corporate licensees (rhs.org.uk), and open APIs like Trefle lack pro-grade regional cultivar rigor. (3) Seasonal Illiquidity (Liquid = 0.1): Northern-climate March-October install rushes restrict CAD adoption to narrow Nov-Feb winter windows, punishing bootstrapped acquisition velocity. *(Indie-favorable: `False`)*

## Blind-Spot Checks

(2) Ecological/native residential planting tailwind: rising demand for native, drought-tolerant, and pollinator gardens—where homeowners buy horticultural expertise and bloom-calendar clarity rather than 3D pool/paver renders—could sustain higher solo-designer WTP and organic community distribution via APLD/SGD/native-plant chapters than hardscape-heavy design-build benchmarks imply. (3) BYO-catalog & nursery-feed bypass: if 80% of target buyers actually reuse the same 150-300 local regional plants and happily import their own nursery CSVs + royalty-free/user-uploaded photos (using a lightweight vector canvas rather than AutoCAD-grade CAD), the 2nd-order 5-region botanical photo copyright moat and engineering scope collapse dramatically, making a US-first indie MVP feasible at far lower fixed cost.
- Advocate: Two symmetric ways this judgment could be wrong: (1) How our KILL / fatal concession could be a false negative: Our competitor audit relies on vendor feature pages and App Store listings (believability 0.4-0.7), NOT live workflow observation with 10+ paying buyers. It is possible—though unlikely given the price gap—that PRO Landscape Contractor's iPad 2D CAD UI is so dated and raster-bound that modern residential planting designers refuse to hand its output to affluent homeowners, that Sow Pro+'s map/photo editor fails completely on scaled 1/8"=1' planting plans and contractor bed-volume takeoffs, AND that Rayon.design's blank-slate horizontal tables require too much CAD setup for non-technical horticulturists—leaving a concentrated, underserved cohort of Mac/iPad landscape design studios willing to pay $600-$900/yr if paired with a low-cost regional nursery data partnership (e.g., co-licensing regional wholesale nursery catalogs in exchange for direct order-sheet attribution rather than scraping RHS/Monrovia).
